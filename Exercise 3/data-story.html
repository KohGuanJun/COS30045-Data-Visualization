2. The Size Factor

The data reveals a hard truth: energy consumption skyrockets as TV size increases from Small to Large. A large TV consumes nearly 5 times the average energy of a small TV. The size of the screen is the absolute biggest driver of your electricity bill.

Moving up just one tier from Medium (50"-55") to Large (65"-75"+) nearly doubles your baseline annual kilowatt draw, adding - annually per display.
PHYSICAL DIMENSIONS

3. OLED's Premium Placement

Furthermore, looking at average screen sizes by technology, OLED TVs are typically manufactured in massive sizes (averaging over 160cm). If you are buying OLED, you are almost certainly buying a very large, power-hungry screen.

  • OLED manufacturers focus almost exclusively on large living room centerpieces (55" to 83").
  • Budget LCDs still dominate smaller kitchen and bedroom form factors (24" to 43").
COMPLETE EMPIRICAL PICTURE

4. The Ultimate Breakdown: Size vs. Tech vs. Energy

This final chart reveals the complete picture. In small TVs, OLED consumes significantly more power than standard LCD. However, in large TVs, the energy gap between LED and OLED is surprisingly minimal.

Final Buying Recommendation

For small bedroom TVs: Avoid OLED to save energy; stick to standard LCD.

For large living room TVs: The energy gap between panel technologies is minimal—so buy only the size you truly need, because size dictates your bill much more than the panel technology!
