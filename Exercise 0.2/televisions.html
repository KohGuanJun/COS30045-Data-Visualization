Television Energy Profiles

Modern displays balance visual fidelity with power draw. Understand how panel technology impacts your household's baseline energy consumption and make informed decisions for long-term efficiency.

Panel Technology Overview

lightbulb

LED

The most mature and common technology. Uses a backlight to illuminate liquid crystals. Generally offers the best baseline energy efficiency due to highly optimized backlighting systems.

display_settings

QLED

An evolution of LED using quantum dots for enhanced color and brightness. Higher peak brightness levels typically result in a moderate increase in power draw compared to standard LEDs.

contrast

OLED

Self-emissive pixels allow for infinite contrast as individual pixels can turn off completely. However, driving bright scenes across the entire panel consumes significantly more energy than backlight technologies.

Efficiency Buying Guide

Key factors to consider beyond the panel type.

aspect_ratio

Screen Size & Resolution

Energy consumption scales nearly exponentially with screen area. A 75-inch TV can use 50% more power than a 65-inch model of the same technology. Similarly, 8K resolution requires significantly more processing and backlight power than 4K.

auto_awesome

HDR Impact

High Dynamic Range (HDR) content requires the TV to push much higher peak brightness levels, noticeably increasing watt usage during viewing compared to Standard Dynamic Range (SDR) content.

energy_savings_leaf

Eco Modes

Look for ambient light sensors that automatically dim the screen in darker rooms, and ensure default 'Eco' picture modes are enabled to cap peak brightness.

Pro Tip

Standby mode (phantom draw) usually accounts for less than 1W on modern TVs, provided quick-start features are disabled.
